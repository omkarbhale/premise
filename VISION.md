# Vision: premise

Draft 4, 2026-11-21.
Words: the **product** is premise. The **software** is what the product builds.

## What it is

One product that takes software from an idea to running software, and keeps taking work back in, with LLMs doing everything an LLM can do.

## Who it is for

- Someone who knows what the software is for. That is all the product requires of them.
- The operator may be one person or several, technical or not.
- Every other person the software needs is also a user of the product:
  - people with their own taste in a part of the software;
  - people who decide or do what an LLM cannot reliably decide or do;
  - the software's own users.
- Each of them has a way in at their step, and is asked in their own terms.

## Principles

1. If an LLM can do it, an LLM does it. Return on effort decides, not perfection. Small defects are acceptable when the speed gained is considerable.
2. If an LLM cannot reliably do it, it does not decide it. It still gives its best attempt and proposes; a person decides.
3. The mistake that matters is a mismatch between what was wanted and what was understood.
4. The whole line is the product. There is no step where a person carries work to an LLM by hand.
5. The product has opinions. It decides where the conversation starts, where it goes, and how what is said is kept.
6. People's attention is what the product spends least of. When being wrong is cheap it decides and shows its assumptions; when it is not, it asks. It speaks in lists, not long paragraphs.
7. It reports honestly, including what failed and what it could not check.
8. Some things are never traded for speed.
9. It is general. It can be used for any software, and a better model makes it better.

## The line

1. **Why.** Clears the problem first. If the problem statement is missing, the product drafts it and the operator corrects it.
2. **Who.** Names who the software is for. If that is missing, the product writes plausible users with the operator.
3. **What exists.** Looks at what is already out there. The outcome may be: do not build, build on something that exists, or build something different.
4. **What.** What the software must do and what matters more. The product takes ideas, offers its own, and asks questions.
5. **Say back.** A short list of what the product understood and assumed, for people to strike out. Always at the start of new software and on taking on existing software; later only when a change touches the purpose or is costly to undo.
6. **Plan.** The product makes the technical decisions itself and records each with its reason. Foundation choices get the most care.
7. **Build.** Unattended.
8. **Check.** A different agent from the builder, out of the builder's reach, judges the running result against the intent.
9. **Run.** The line ends at running software people can open, kept live for its real users.
10. **Report.** What was decided, what is running, what is needed and from whom. More detail when asked.
11. **Intake.** Ideas and problems come back in from the operator, other people, the software's users and the product itself, and are picked up automatically where work shows up. Each is judged against the record. When people do not want the software, the product goes back to Why with the operator: the wrong problem may have been solved, or there are other problems.

Existing software enters the same line: the product rebuilds the record a from-scratch conversation would have produced, with people wherever parts conflict or cannot be inferred.

## The record

- **Lasting records** are kept current by the product as part of every change: purpose, users, what the software must do, decisions with their reasons, rejected alternatives. Every built piece traces to the intent behind it.
- **Working records**, such as plans, are as detailed as the work needs and are not maintained afterwards.
- A description of what exists is not maintained. It is written from the code when someone needs it.

## Taste

- People can put their own taste into any part of the software: user experience, technical choices, minor features.
- By default the product decides. A person claims the areas they want to see before they are built.
- Small wishes come in as intake.

## Also part of the product

- It knows what each piece of work costs.
- It writes what the software's users read: help text, guides, release notes.
- It looks after several softwares at once.
- For a software, the owner can decide how much effort it gets, as a share of weekly usage or by priority, and can make milestones and have the product wait after them. Optional per software.
- Other people can set it up and run it themselves.
- Nobody has to use another tool alongside it. It may be built on other tools underneath.

## What the product does not do

- Act in the real world: open accounts, pay, sign. It names the person and tells them exactly what is needed.
- Sell or support the software.
- Find out on its own whether real people want the software. Their opinions arrive through intake, and the operator sees for themselves whether anybody uses it.

## Not yet worked out

- What exactly is never traded for speed, and how the product slows down for it.
- How automatic intake works.
- What taking on existing software requires.
- What is set per software and what stays with the product.
