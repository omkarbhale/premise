## Settled

- Scope spans ideas and decisions through properly built software, including intake.
- The operator relationship is two-way: the factory draws ideas and decisions out of the operator, accepts ideas, offers ideas, and asks questions.
- Implementation is free: any means that supports the above. Existing tools (entail, firstmate, deploys, usage-ledger) are candidates, not the starting point.
- Governing principle: whatever an LLM can do, an LLM does; the test is ROI, not perfection. Defects are acceptable when the speed gain is considerable.
- Target operator: a single person who knows the purpose of the software.

- The whole line is the product, not only the conversation: no step where a person must manually hand work to an LLM. The product is opinionated about where the conversation starts and goes, and about how its records are separated, grouped and persisted (e.g. problem statement cleared first; plausible target users drafted with the operator when undefined).
- Reporting to the operator is part of the line, at the level of detail the operator wants or at the product's own default.
- What LLMs cannot reliably do is not given to LLMs: the factory says which other people are needed. One operator only is not strict, and the operator is not strictly non-technical; design target remains a person who knows the purpose.
- The mistake that matters is a mismatch between the intent wanted and the intent inferred; small bugs are tolerable.
- Ends at running software, not merged code.
- Covers existing software as well as new. Automatic intake is wanted.
- Coarse cost tracking is in (ROI needs a cost side).

- Everyone needed is a user of the product: operator(s) and every other role, one person or many; the product gives each a way in at their step.
- Where an LLM cannot reliably decide, it still gives its best attempt and proposes; a person decides.
- The product guards against false reports, wrong early foundations and silent contradictions, and any further big-mistake source found later.
- Existing software goes through the same line, with people where parts conflict or cannot be inferred.
- People can assert their own taste on parts of the software; minor wishes can be ticket intake; review-before-build can be chosen per area. Shape left open on purpose (avoid premature design).
- Play-back rule and reporting default agreed (see statements).

- Looking at what already exists is a step of the line: for one operator it saves effort in part or in full (including not building at all), for another it is finding what others are missing. It applies to both new and existing software. Not yet on the review page; add it as a step between Who and What when the page is next revised, and split edge item E2 (the research half is LLM work; whether real users want it still needs people).

- Two kinds of record. Lasting records (purpose, users, what it must do, decisions with reasons, rejected alternatives) are kept current by the product as part of every change. Working records (plans and the like, meant for immediate use) can be detailed and thorough and are not maintained.
- People's attention is the thing to spend least of: decide and show assumptions when being wrong is cheap; the say-back is a short list of assumptions to strike out. Lists over long paragraphs when the product speaks to people (owner's opinion "for now").
- The check is independent of the builder and out of its reach; its form is free (an agent's review pass or anything else).
- The product is general: usable for any software, with its prompts and logic already decided. What is set per software at run time versus what stays with the product is to be decided. Quality may depend on the models used.

- A description of what exists (technical architecture, code documentation) is not maintained: it is regenerated from the code on demand; only the why is kept. The check is a different agent from the builder, not editable by it, judging the running result against the intent. The product's logic is about the line and the records, not workarounds for one model. (Owner 2026-11-20: "I agree on your points.")

- Name: premise. It stands alone: it does whatever of entail's work it needs itself, and nobody has to use both.
