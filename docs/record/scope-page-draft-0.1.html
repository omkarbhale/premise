The software factory

The whole line, from the first conversation to running software and back round, with every part open to be marked in, later or out.

Words used here: the product is the factory being made. The software is what the product builds.

What it is

Taken from what has been said so far. Annotate any card that is wrong.

The whole line is the product

From ideas and decisions to running software, and intake back in. No join where a person carries work to an LLM by hand.

If an LLM can do it, an LLM does it

Return on effort decides, not perfection. Small defects are acceptable when the speed gain is considerable.

If an LLM cannot reliably do it, it does not decide it

It still reasons and gives its best attempt. A person with the right role decides.

The mistake that matters is a mismatch of intent

What was wanted against what was understood. So the product says back what it understood, traces every built piece to an intent, and shows running software early.

The product has opinions

It decides where the conversation starts, where it goes next, and how what is said is split up and kept.

Everyone needed is a user of the product

The operator, who may be more than one person, and every other role. Each has a way in at their step.

It reports honestly

At a default level set by the product, deeper on request. A false report, a wrong early foundation and a silent contradiction are guarded against.

It needs someone who knows the purpose

That is the design target. The operator may be technical, and may have their own taste, but the product does not depend on it.

The line

How work moves. The top row is the conversation, the bottom row runs on its own, and the record in the middle is what both rows answer to.

A person is asked or decides at this step. Everywhere else the product proceeds without anyone carrying the work. A person can still claim any step for their own taste.

Along the line

The steps in the picture above.

L1

Clears the problem first

your word

Before anything else the product gets the reason for the software stated: what problem, and why build this. If it is missing, the product drafts it and the operator corrects it.

L2

Names who the software is for

your word

If the software's users are not defined, the product writes plausible ones with the operator's help.

L3

What it must do, and what matters more

your word

Outcomes in purpose terms and the priorities between them. Here the product takes ideas, offers its own, and asks questions.

L4

Says back what it understood

your word

Always at the start of new software and when taking on existing software. For later changes, only when the change touches the purpose or would be costly to undo.

L5

Makes the technical decisions itself

your word

Each decision is recorded with its reason. Early foundation choices get more care, because a wrong one makes everything after it expensive.

L6

Builds without anyone carrying the work

your word

Nobody goes to a chat window to ask for code. Running work in parallel, landing it, and keeping pieces from colliding are the product's job.

L7

Proves the build against the record

your word

Checks the running thing against what was wanted and says truthfully what passed, what failed and what it could not check. A false “it works” is one of the worst failures.

L8

Gets it running where people can open it

your word

The line ends at running software, not at merged code.

L9

Shows and reports

your word

Product default: what was decided, what is running, what it needs and from whom. More detail when someone asks for it.

L10

Takes ideas and problems back in

your word

From the operator, from other people, and the product's own ideas. Small wishes arrive as tickets. Each one is judged against the record, then goes back to What, or to Why if it changes the purpose.

L11

Picks up work without being told

your word

From the running software and from wherever work shows up: issue trackers, error reports, user feedback. How this works is parked for the whole plan.

L12

Takes on software that already exists

your word

Rebuilds the same record a from-scratch conversation would have produced, with people wherever parts conflict or cannot be inferred. What this needs is parked for the whole plan.

My opinion on order: after new software works end to end.

Across the whole line

Things every step depends on.

A1

One record that everything traces to

your word

Why, who, what and every decision, with each built piece linked to the intent behind it. A mismatch can be located, and a later decision is checked against earlier ones so nothing is silently undone.

A2

Everyone needed is a user of the product

your word

The operator or operators, anyone with taste in an area, and people named for what an LLM cannot reliably do. One person or many. Each gets a way in at their step and is asked in their own terms.

A3

Best attempt where it cannot decide

your word

Where an LLM cannot reliably decide, it still reasons, gives its best attempt and proposes. A person decides.

A4

People can put their own taste in

your word

User experience, technical choices, minor features. Minor wishes come in as tickets; review before building can be chosen for the areas someone cares about.

Shape left open on purpose. See question 1 below.

A5

Knows what things cost

your word

Coarse cost per piece of work, so return on effort is measured and not guessed.

A6

Things never traded for speed

your word

Lost data, a security hole, money spent, something sent in a person's name, a false report. Agreed in principle; how it works is parked for the whole plan.

At the edges

You have not spoken to these. Each is a real choice about where the product stops.

E1

Keeps the software live for its real users

my suggestion

Hosting, staying up, incidents, updates reaching users. The alternative for a first version: “running” means a place the people working with the product can open and try.

E2

Finds out whether real users want it

my suggestion

The product can suggest and reason, but only real users can answer. Needs people outside the product.

E3

Acts in the real world

my suggestion

Opening accounts, paying, licences, signatures. The product names the person and tells them exactly what is needed.

E4

Writes what the software's users read

my suggestion

Help text, a user guide, release notes.

E5

Sells and supports the software

my suggestion

Marketing, pricing, customer support.

E6

Runs several softwares at once

my suggestion

One copy of the product looking after more than one software.

E7

Other people can set the product up

my suggestion

Someone other than you installing and running it on their own machine. Designed for from the start, proven on your work first.

E8

Builds any kind of software

my suggestion

Web, desktop, mobile, add-ons to platforms. Not restricted in principle; the first version proves itself on one or two kinds you choose.

Where I think this can go wrong

My own view, for you to argue with.

The conversation turns into paperwork

Too many questions up front lose the speed the product exists for. The product should draft and let people correct, not interview.

A say-back that confirms the wrong thing

People agree with plausible text without reading it closely. Running software shown early is the real check; the say-back is only the cheap first one.

Existing software swallows the effort

Taking on what already exists is a larger problem than building new. If it comes first, the line may never be proven end to end.

The whole line is a lot

Every part being in the product does not mean every part is built at once. After scope, the next decision is the order.
